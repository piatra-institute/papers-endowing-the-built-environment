# Renewal model protocol

Frozen before comparative results, 2026-10-10. This is a conditional accounting
and timing model. There are no estimated failure probabilities or market forecasts.

All cash is in constant purchasing-power euros. Construction costs grow relative
to that numeraire; returns are real, net of investment costs but before the common
steward fee. Apartment net rent excludes its own operating, interior-renewal and
operating-tax costs; common building work and the steward fee remain below it.
Project tax is outside the launch example. No leverage, appreciation, rent growth,
cross-subsidy or rescue is implicit.

Compare equal EUR 3.3m dedicated capital: a liquid conventional reserve earning
2% real, a liquid financial endowment earning 4%, and 25 apartments valued at
EUR 3m plus EUR 0.3m cash earning 2%. Include a conventional reserve with the
identical 4% portfolio as an equivalence control. A better return is an assumption
about investment, not an effect of the word endowment. Every treatment serves
the same whole-building obligations and starts with the same component condition.

Each year: age components; credit returns and rental income; pay routine work and
the fee; consider due interventions in fixed component order. If cash is
insufficient, pay only from explicit owner calls or authorized whole-apartment
sales in those separately labelled policies; otherwise record arrears. Due
component work waits until fully funded. Delayed components remain overdue;
replacement resets age at actual execution, not scheduled execution. Negative
operating balances remain visible as unsecured arrears and receive no interest.
An overdue component-year is a service-risk proxy, not an observed outage.

At each annual review project the next 20 years with current component ages,
timely work, baseline future returns and rent, and the scenario's known cost
trend. Calculate the minimum cash needed at that date from the maximum discounted
cumulative deficit. Forecasts do not anticipate one-time shocks. At year 60 report
this continuation requirement and liquid funding gap separately from rental
property value. Future rent and the capitalized value of that rent are not both
credited to meet the same obligation.

Deterministic stresses: 35% financial loss in year 19 or 21; same-building roof
failure in year 12 plus 50% rental income in years 12-14; construction growth
3%; doubled fees; two-year approval delay; a combined stress. Cash return is
unchanged by an equity loss. Every physical/approval shock affects all schemes.
Run no-call/no-sale, explicit calls, and rental-unit liquidation variants. Compare
present value of all owner calls at 2% real. Report sensitivity to 0%, 2% and 4%
financial returns; solve the least additional opening cash for timely works plus
terminal continuation under each baseline scheme. Do not optimize inputs after
seeing which scheme wins.

Required outputs: launch accounting, integer feasibility, annual ledgers, paired
scenario comparisons, overdue work, maximum cash arrears, extra calls and their
present value, rental units sold, terminal continuation gaps, required initial
capital, and a separately labelled hypothetical steward break-even calculation.
